1. The exam at a glance

Questions115 scored: 80 national + 35 Michigan (plus 5–10 unscored "experimental" ones that don't count)
To pass70%, which is 80 of the 115
Time180 minutes (3 hours). That's over 1.5 minutes a question, which is plenty
Who runs itPSI, at a test center. You get only PASS/FAIL, no number
To get licensedFinish the 40-hour pre-licensing course (at least 4 hours on civil rights/fair housing), pass this exam, apply in MiPLUS, and have a sponsoring broker

3. Math cheat sheet

Only about 6 questions are pure math, but the same math shows up inside other questions. These are all of the formulas the outline lists.

Commission

commission = sale price × rate · a split takes a share of that

Seller's net proceeds

net = sale price − commission − loan payoff − seller's closing costs − transfer tax

Buyer's cash at closing

cash = price + buyer's closing costs − loan − earnest money − credits

Prorating (taxes, rent, interest)

daily amount = yearly amount ÷ 365 (some problems say to use 360 or 12 thirty-day months, so do what the question says) · × number of days owed

Loans

LTV = loan ÷ value · 1 point = 1% of the LOAN (not the price) · PITI = principal&interest + (yearly tax ÷ 12) + (yearly insurance ÷ 12)

Investment

cap rate = NOI ÷ value · value = NOI ÷ cap rate · equity = value − what's owed

Land

square feet = length × width · 1 acre = 43,560 sq ft · 1 section = 1 square mile = 640 acres · 1 township = 36 sections

Michigan transfer tax (check in your course)

Michigan charges a state tax and a county tax when property is sold, worked out per $500 of price, and the seller usually pays it. Get the exact current rates from your course, because a rate is exactly the kind of number that changes.

5. Michigan checklist

These are the Michigan headings straight from the PSI outline. Tick them off in your head as your course covers each one.

  • Elliott-Larsen Civil Rights Act, and the Persons with Disabilities Civil Rights Act (Michigan's fair housing laws)
  • Land Division Act (private road disclosure)
  • Landlord-Tenant Relationship Act, and the Truth in Renting Act
  • State transfer tax
  • Michigan Condominium Act
  • MSHDA (Michigan State Housing Development Authority)
  • Uniform State Antitrust Act
  • Right to Farm Act (the seller has to disclose it)
  • Land contract law changes (Dodd-Frank)
  • Stigmatized property: in Michigan there's no duty to disclose it
  • Agency types available in Michigan: transaction coordinator, designated agency, dual agency, and the disclosures for each
  • Advertising rules, commissions, trust money, record keeping, inducements, closing duties
  • Licensing: what needs a license, license types, renewal and continuing education, transfers

6. Study plan for 60 days

  • Weeks 1–3: your pre-licensing course. After each module, flip the matching flashcards here.
  • Week 4: take a full practice exam, then look at which sections of part 2 you lost points in.
  • Weeks 5–6: practice questions on your weakest two sections, plus the math until it's automatic.
  • The last week: two timed practice exams, then the Michigan checklist one more time.
  • Book the exam once you're steadily scoring 80%+ on practice tests. That leaves you a cushion over the 70% you need.

7. Free (and cheap) extras

  • Your course's practice exams come first. Use every quiz in it.

  • Free question banks and video channels are everywhere, but most are written for the national part only. Whatever you use, also do the Michigan checklist above. I haven't vetted particular ones, so ask your course or your broker which ones they trust before spending money.
  • A paid extra worth considering after the course: a Michigan-specific practice exam bank. It's usually cheaper than a second textbook and closer to the real test.
